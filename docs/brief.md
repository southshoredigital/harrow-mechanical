# Discovery brief: Harrow Mechanical

**Status:** Concept project. Harrow Mechanical is a fictional business created to demonstrate process and output. Any resemblance to a real firm is coincidental.
**Package:** Studio, notional $8,000
**Date:** [build date]

---

## The business

Harrow Mechanical is a commercial mechanical services contractor based in Dandenong South, founded 1994, 58 staff. They design, install, commission and maintain HVAC, hydraulic and building control systems for commercial buildings.

**Sectors served:** health, education, data centres, industrial process, commercial office.

**Typical contract value:** $400k to $4.2m. Maintenance contracts $40k to $180k a year.

**How they win work:** roughly 70% competitive tender through head contractors, 20% negotiated repeat work with the same builders, 10% direct approach from facility owners who have had them before.

---

## Who actually looks at the site

Three audiences, in order of commercial value. This ordering drives the entire structure.

1. **Head contractor estimators and project managers**, checking whether Harrow is credible enough to invite to tender. They are looking for: relevant project history at similar scale, accreditations, insurance currency, capability in the specific system type, and whether the firm looks like it will still exist in two years. They spend under three minutes.
2. **Facility and asset managers** with a problem, usually searching after hours. They want to know Harrow services their building type and how to reach someone.
3. **Prospective employees**, particularly refrigeration mechanics and controls technicians. The trade shortage is real and Harrow loses candidates to firms with better presentation.

**What none of them want:** a mission statement, a stock photo of a hard hat, or a hero video of a city skyline.

---

## The problem with the current situation

- The existing site is a five page template from 2016 with a services list and a contact form.
- Their project history, which is genuinely their strongest asset, exists only as a PDF capability statement emailed on request.
- Accreditations are named in body copy rather than displayed.
- No careers presence at all, despite six open roles.
- Nothing is indexed for the system-type searches facility managers actually use.
- On a phone, which is where the site office looks at it, it is close to unusable.

## What it's costing

Their words: they get invited to tender on reputation and relationships, and they suspect they are being passed over by newer head contractors who have never heard of them and who look them up before deciding who to invite. They cannot prove that, which is itself part of the problem.

---

## Commercial framing

- Average contract $900k, gross margin roughly 12%, so about $108k of gross profit per job.
- One additional tender invitation converting once every two years pays for this many times over.
- Framing for the proposal: this is not a marketing cost, it is a pre-qualification asset.

---

## What the site must do

**Primary conversion action:** an estimator downloads or views the capability statement and adds Harrow to a tender list.

**Secondary:** a facility manager makes contact. **Tertiary:** a tradesperson applies.

### Required structure

- **Projects** — filterable by sector, system type and contract scale. This is the most important part of the site and should be treated as the centrepiece, not a gallery tucked in the footer.
- **Capabilities** — by system, not by marketing category. Mechanical, hydraulic, controls and BMS, commissioning, maintenance.
- **Accreditations and compliance** — displayed, current, with a document library holding insurance certificates, licences, ISO certification, safety statistics and SWMS templates.
- **Careers** — real roles, real people, plain language about what the work is.
- **About** — 30 years of continuity is the asset. Say it with numbers.
- **Contact** — separate paths for tender enquiry, service call and careers. One generic form is a failure.

### Explicitly out of scope

Blog, news feed, social integration, live chat. They will not maintain any of it and an abandoned news page dated 2027 is worse than no news page.

---

## Content

Client supplies: project list with photography, accreditation documents, staff numbers and safety statistics, role descriptions.
Southshore develops: all page copy, project case study write-ups, meta content.
Photography: existing plant room and site photography, colour graded to a single treatment. No stock.

---

## Constraints

- Must be legible on a phone in bright light on site. This is a real usage condition, not a nicety.
- Document library must be updatable by their office manager without developer involvement.
- No client logos displayed without written permission, which is standard in head contractor relationships.

---

## What success looks like

At six months: measurable traffic from system-type searches, capability statement views tracked as a conversion, and at least one tender invitation the client can attribute to the site. Set up the tracking to answer that question, since currently nobody can.
